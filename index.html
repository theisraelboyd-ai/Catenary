How the model works

Using it

  • Set the cable and chute on the left, then add the bottom tensions you want to compare.
  • Pick a water depth with the depth bar, the + and − buttons, or by tapping either chart. Every view follows that depth.
  • Dashed parts of a curve are shallower than that tension's critical water depth (CWD). The hatched area is where any tension would be critical.
  • Use Solve from what you see with the depth and either an observed layback or angle. The matching tension is drawn as a dashed curve and can be added to the comparison.
  • Settings are kept on this device between visits.

The catenary and parameter a

A cable hanging under its own weight forms a catenary. Its shape is set by a = H / q, where H is the horizontal tension (constant along the span, and equal to the tension at the touchdown point) and q is the weight per metre. Higher tension gives a flatter curve and a longer layback.

y = a · [cosh(x / a) − 1]

Two segments: water then air

The submerged span from touchdown to the surface uses the weight in water. The span from the surface up to the chute exit uses the weight in air. The two are joined at the sea surface with matching tension and slope.

What each value means

  • Layback: horizontal distance from the chute exit to the touchdown point.
  • Length: cable from touchdown to the chute exit, plus the length wrapped on the chute. The wrap term, r·(90° − θ), is deliberately not the pure geometric wrap: it allows for depth, chute height and cable stiffness, and was checked against OASYS to within about a metre. Don't change it without re-validating.
  • Angle at surface: cable angle from vertical where it crosses the sea surface.
  • Angle at chute: cable angle from vertical where it leaves the chute (the departure point). Slightly closer to vertical than the surface angle, typically by about half a degree. Compare like with like: OASYS and on-deck readings at the chute go against this one.
  • Tensioner load: top tension √(H² + V²) at the chute, reduced by chute friction: T / e^(μ·θ).
T_tensioner = √(H² + V²) / e^(μ·θ)

Critical water depth

Below the CWD for a given tension, the air segment's weight dominates and small vessel movements cause large tension changes. CWD is a fitted curve from the reference below, with H in tonnes:

CWD = 0.0698H⁴ − 1.2321H³ + 6.5376H² + 2.9029H + 3.1488

Reference

Mamatsopoulos et al. (2020). Critical Water Depth and Installation Curves for Submarine Cable Deployment Process. JMSE 8(11), 838. doi.org/10.3390/jmse8110838
